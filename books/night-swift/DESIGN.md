# Мира и стрижонок

## Premise

Ночью на чердак падает стрижонок, который ещё ни разу не летал. Котёнок Мира
должна помочь ему вернуться к маме до рассвета, хотя стрижи не умеют
взлетать с пола.

## High concept

Кошка, которая спасает птицу: котёнок учится быть терпеливым и ведёт
испуганного птенца по своей тайной дороге — со стула на стол и на
подоконник, откуда стрижонок сможет упасть в небо.

Reader fantasy: being the small, clever one who helps someone even smaller,
using a route through the room only Mira knows.

## Audience

Ages 5–9. An adult reads aloud; a confident eight-year-old can read alone.
Short sentences, concrete words, gentle peril only. One real fact about
swifts carries the story: they cannot take off from flat ground and need a
high place to drop from.

## Tone

Warm night-time fairy tale with gentle humour. Tension comes from the
fledgling's fear and the coming dawn, never from violence. Mira is a cat and
feels the urge to pounce; choosing patience instead is the heart of the book.

## Hero

Mira, the grey storybook kitten with white socks (Scener
`prefabs/characters/mira.blk`), third person. She reads in wide shots by her
light fur against the dark floor and her large pointed ears.

Supporting characters:

- **The fledgling (стрижонок)**, dark grey-brown, long wings it cannot use
  yet, a wide beak, frightened. States: hidden, curious, trusting, brave.
- **The mother swift**, heard and glimpsed outside the window, screaming in
  circles above the roof. States: unseen (heard), seen at the window, reunited.
- **Grandmother**, asleep in her armchair by the lamp after reading. States:
  asleep, stirring, awake and helping (only in one ending).

## Setting and time

Grandmother's attic study, from about three
in the morning until first light. Light moves from warm lamplight and cold
moonlight to the pale blue of dawn in the last act, so the colour script
follows the clock.

## Acts

1. **The noise (exploring).** Mira hears peeping, searches the attic, and
   finds the fledgling hiding behind the trunk. Threshold: she sees the bird.
2. **Trust and the idea (understanding).** Pouncing frightens it under the
   bookshelf; lying still and purring earns its trust. A bird book open on the
   desk and the mother's calls through the window give the idea: swifts need
   height. Threshold: the fledgling hops after Mira.
3. **The climb (action).** Mira leads it chair → desk → sill, deciding at each
   step whether to nudge or wait. At the sill, the window is only ajar.
   Threshold: the fledgling drops from the sill into the dawn.

After act 1 the attic floor pages show the fledgling; after act 2 the desk
and sill pages show the dawn beginning and the mother at the window.

## Size and painting budget

Three locations (floor, desk, sill), each with states before and after the
fledgling follows Mira: about 8 room pages, 10 beat pages and 2 endings, so
about 20 paintings plus the cover.

## Structure

Branch and bottleneck. Act 1 lets the reader search in any order; act 2
branches on pounce or patience and rejoins; act 3 is a gauntlet of three
jumps, each with a nudge-or-wait choice, ending in a two-way final decision.

## Goal and endings

Good ending (goal): the fledgling drops from the sill, catches the air and
flies up to its mother as the sky turns pink. Tested by the fact
`FLEDGLING_FLEW`.

- **Mira's ending:** Mira pushes the window wider with her head; the
  fledgling launches; Mira watches the two swifts circle the roof.
- **Grandmother's ending:** Mira wakes grandmother, who opens the window wide
  and lifts the fledgling to the sill; grandmother and Mira watch together.

Both are good endings with different warmth. Pouncing or hurrying never ends
the book; they cause setbacks that the text turns into lessons.

## Opening page

Landmark: grandmother asleep in her armchair under the lamp. Visible goal: a
faint peeping from somewhere in the dark attic. Obstacle: Mira does not know
where it comes from. First success: one choice later she finds a small grey
feather on the floor, pointing the way.

## Contrast and secrets

- Funny: Mira checks her own basket first and finds only her yarn ball; the
  fledgling opens its huge beak at Mira expecting food.
- Beautiful: the swifts wheeling against the dawn; moonlight on the sill.
- Warm: the fledgling falling asleep against Mira's fur; grandmother's hand on
  Mira's head in her ending.
- Secret: if Mira first looks at the bird book on the desk, a later page lets
  her recognise the mother's call and choose the right moment.

## Exclusions

No inventory, no carried items, no collecting, no dice or timers, no typed
commands, no sound. Mira never harms the bird; pouncing only makes it hide.
