# Мира и лунный зайчик

A deliberately short example book for the authoring guide: one room, two play
areas, thirteen pages, two good endings. Every stage document for it exists, so
it can be read next to [authoring/README.md](../../authoring/README.md) as a
worked example.

## Premise

Mira the kitten wants to sleep in the round patch of moonlight trembling on the
kitchen dresser, but a moonbeam cannot be caught by pouncing.

## High concept

A kitten works out where light comes from: the reader stops chasing a spot of
moonlight and learns to steer it with grandmother's mirror, then decides whose
bed it should warm.

Reader fantasy: being clever with something everybody has chased and nobody
has caught.

## Audience

Ages 4–8. An adult reads aloud; a six-year-old can read alone. Short sentences,
everyday words, one sound word per page at most ("хлоп", "апчхи"). No danger
beyond a grumpy old dog.

## Tone

Quiet, warm, a little silly. A bedtime book: the pictures stay calm and dim,
the jokes are physical (a kitten stuck on a curtain, a dog sneezing), and it
ends with two animals asleep. No bad endings.

## Hero

Mira, the grey storybook kitten with white socks and large pointed ears.
Third person. She reads in wide
shots by her pale fur against the dark floorboards and her ear silhouette.

Supporting character:

- **Бублик**, grandmother's old shaggy mongrel, sand-coloured with a dark
  muzzle and one floppy ear. States: asleep and snoring, one eye open and
  grumbling, sitting up and suspicious, friendly, asleep with Mira.

## Setting and time

Grandmother's village kitchen, around midnight. The only lights are a low moon
through the north window and the red glow of the stove's fire door, so every
page mixes cold silver and warm red. Time does not pass visibly; the story takes
a few minutes.

## Acts

1. **Chasing (exploring).** Mira tries to catch the moon-spot on the dresser
   door, wakes Бублик, and climbs the stool to the table, where she sees the
   beam coming from grandmother's mirror. Threshold: she pushes the mirror.
2. **Understanding.** The spot jumps onto Бублик's nose; he wakes with a sneeze.
   Now Mira knows the rule: where the mirror turns, the spot goes. The floor
   and the table both change (the dog sits up, the spot lies on his mat, the
   table offers aiming). Threshold: Mira decides where the spot should sleep.
3. **Deciding (ending).** Either she steers the spot into her own basket, or
   she lies down with Бублик in the spot already on his mat.

## Size and budget

One location (the kitchen) with two play areas, floor and table. Four room
pages, seven beats, two endings: thirteen paintings plus the cover. Reading
time three to five minutes.

## Structure

Hub. Mira moves freely between the floor and the table; two facts decide what
each page offers. It is the smallest structure that still has a clue chain,
a character who reacts, a world change and a meaningful last choice.

## Goal and endings

Good ending (goal): Mira falls asleep inside the moon-spot. Both endings reach
it; they differ in whom she chose.

- **The basket ending** (`kitchen-sleep-basket`): after pushing the mirror
  once, Mira turns it toward her basket and sleeps in her own bed. If Бублик
  told her where the spot comes from, he mumbles "I told you" in his sleep.
- **Бублик's ending** (`kitchen-sleep-bublik`): after the spot lands on his
  mat and he has invited her, Mira curls up against his warm side in the spot.
  The old photograph by the window pays off here.

Sequel hook: "tomorrow the sun will look in, and a sun-spot is warmer still."

## Opening page

Landmark: the dresser with the moon-spot on its lower door. Visible goal: the
spot itself; the text says Mira wants to sleep in it. Obstacle: it trembles and
slips away. First success: within one choice the reader learns something real
(the spot slides onto Mira's back; Бублик says it comes from the window) and
the stool offers the way up.

## Contrast and secrets

| Act | Funny | Beautiful | Warm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mira pounces and the spot lands on her own back | the beam crossing the dark kitchen from the table | Бублик's grumpy invitation |
| 2 | Бублик sneezes the spot off his nose; Mira hangs on the curtain like a sock | the spot sliding across the floorboards | Бублик admits he chased spots as a puppy |
| 3 | Бублик mumbling in his sleep | the basket full of silver light | two animals asleep in one patch of moonlight |

Secret: the framed photograph by the window shows puppy Бублик chasing a
sun-spot. It is painted from the first page on, and only Бублик's ending
mentions it.

Alternative routes: talk to Бублик before or after the push; reach either
ending; the pounce and curtain jokes can be skipped entirely.

## Exclusions

No inventory, no carrying, no collecting, no timers or dice, no typed commands,
no sound. Nobody is hurt; pouncing only teaches. Grandmother never appears; she
is asleep in the next room and present only through her things.
