# The Salt Ledger

Grayhaven, 1924. One rainy night. Edith Voss hires the detective Harlan Crowe
to read her brother Thomas's hour aloud in her dry office, and to leave the
shack floor at berth four shut.

Read this file before adding a page, a fact, a picture, or a clip. The played
prose is `title.xml` and `locations/*.xml`. The screens and the circles are [GDD.md](GDD.md).
When this file and that one disagree about a plate, follow GDD.md and fix
this file. The routes are [WALKTHROUGH.md](WALKTHROUGH.md). The map, the one
object, the puzzle, and the facts are in `work/`.

## Premise

Thomas Voss, a tide clerk of twenty-eight, went down to berth four and did not
come back. The harbor ledger in Caleb Marsh's shack has his name, an hour
already passed, and a line in Edith's hand: say it where the floor is dry.
Under the shack floor is the water. Thomas answers if his hour is spoken where
the water can hear it.

What is wrong here:

- Thomas is not lost at sea. He stands under the streetlamp, turns under the
  pilings, and waits beneath the floor. The changed face keeps his face.
- The hour in the ledger is a summons. Spoken in the rain, on the dock, or
  under the floor, it calls him. Spoken in the office, onto Edith's folder, it
  lets the tide go out unanswered.
- Marsh will say this. He will not touch the book, and he will not go down the
  trap.

## High concept

Look, listen, and decide where a dead man's name is allowed to be spoken.

The reader is hired help with a coat and a lamp, working one rule a client
already wrote in the margin. The special pleasure is noticing that the harbor
is listening, and then choosing a mouth for the hour.

## Journey line

The night starts in the office, with Edith in the chair. The goal is the same
room, later, with the ledger open on her folder. Between them is the hill, the
lane, and berth four: the Patience, Marsh, the shack, and the water under it.

The opposing force is the harbor. It does not chase. It waits where a name can
be spoken: the glass, the fog, the pilings, the trap. The reason to be afraid
is on the first pages. Edith says leave the floor shut, and the streetlamp
across the rain already has a man in it.

Strangers: Edith, who hires and waits; Marsh, who keeps the boat and will not
touch the book; Thomas, who is the man, the face, and the step in the fog.

This night is one harbor cluster, three places and a lane. It is a finished
case. A longer adventure, in the sense of [authoring/CRAFT.md](../../authoring/CRAFT.md),
would leave the hill for other streets. Those streets need their own plates.

## Audience

One reader, about half an hour, played again for the ending they missed.
Adult. The sentences are short enough to read under a picture. Danger is real
and cold. It is never gory: no wounds, no blood, no body count. A failure
tells you what you did and offers another try. The dull ending is going home
with empty hands and finding the folder still on the desk in the morning.

## Tone and voice

Mystery first. The first pages can be an ordinary missing brother. The
supernatural turn is cold and late: a man too still for the rain, a face the
size of a face between the pilings, a latch that rises under a hand that is
not yours.

The beauty is the harbor at night. Wet wood, coal smoke, a green glass lamp,
black water, a streetlamp that is either occupied or empty. Name those things.
Leave the dread in how still they are.

Voice, kept on every new page:

- Second person, present tense. "You cross to the glass." Crowe is "you"
  throughout. He is never named on the page.
- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. One action, then what it means.
- Feelings show up as a hand on a latch, a palm between a book and a deck, a
  locket closing. They are not explained.
- Spoken lines are short and attributed by the act. Edith states the rule
  once. Marsh is dry: "The rain is not a room." Thomas does not give a speech.
- Humour is one dry gesture from Marsh. There are no jokes.
- A failure says what you did. "You read his hour to the harbor." It does not
  scold.
- The best ending remembers the road: the streetlamp stays empty, and the
  floor you left shut does not answer. The quiet ending, if the lamp and the
  face and Marsh were skipped, says the rain is only rain.

Leave these unsaid. The book is smaller and colder for the gap:

- No species, god, cult, or spell name. Thomas keeps his face. Lidless eyes
  and gill slits are the whole change.
- No lecture on how a summons works beyond the four clues below.
- No daylight, no second city, no newspaper account of the night.
- No score. The ending is the score.

Edith is warm and plain. She wears the locket and she trusts the room. Marsh
is the harbor's employee, not a friend and not a villain. He looks at the
shack with the clouded eye. Crowe is polite, hired, and careful. He does not
boast and he does not run.

## Hero

Harlan Crowe, 41, 183 cm. Seated eyes at 120 cm, standing eyes at 160 cm.
Charcoal overcoat, dark fedora, mustache. On the stills and in the clips he is
the camera. `concepts/crowe.jpg` keeps his face if a photograph or a reflection
is ever required. A clip shows what he sees. Edith, Marsh, and Thomas move.
He does not walk into the shot, sit for a two-shot, or put his hand on a door
in the film.

Reach is a standing man's. The rope in the shack floor is in reach from where
he stands. He does not climb, jump, or swim. Kneeling at the edge of the dock
is as far as the water gets him.

## Setting, route and time

Grayhaven is a harbor town on one wet night. The office looks down a rainy
street. The lane ends at berth four. The shack sits on that dock, over the
water. The tide in the ledger has already passed. The hour does not expire
while the reader thinks. Rain and the green lamp stay; do not advance the
clock to morning except in the dull ending, which is told, not shown.

The route, in the order a careful reader walks it:

1. Office, Edith present, then the folder and the window.
2. Down the hill to berth four.
3. The water, Marsh on the Patience, the shack and the ledger.
4. Back up the lane with the book shut: the alley, the sluice, the landing.
5. The same office, Edith in the chair. The hour goes onto the folder.

Branches leave that line for the fog, the glass, the trap, or the walk home.

## Acts

**Departure.** Edith in the chair, then the empty office. Activity: take the
job and read the margin. Threshold: the door opens only after the folder.
Afterwards the hill is allowed, and the rule is in writing.

**The harbor.** Berth four. Activity: look, listen, take the book, leave the
floor shut. Threshold: the lane with the ledger shut in the coat. Afterwards
the dry room is the last choice, and holding the book open on the dock can
spend the hour.

**The town.** The shut book, from the pier to the office. Three screens: the
alley, the sluice, the landing. Activity: look, ask, and refuse the water.
Threshold: the hall, and the dry room on the other side. The board, Penry,
and the peg change what the road says. They do not join the best ending.

**The dry room.** The office again. Activity: choose the mouth. The folder is
the win. The glass is the last wrong mouth. The door only goes back down.

## Size

What is built: the office, the wharf, Marsh's deck, the shack, the alley, the
sluice, the landing, the dry room, and the bend where the hill starts. A hub
is a still with circles. A close-up or a clip is one beat, then back to the
hub. No two of those beats chain. Eleven endings (two successes, one partial,
eight failures). One scene. Sixteen clips.

A new place is a new camera and a new still. This night does not grow by
pointing a new page at an existing JPEG.

## Structure

A hub inside one night, then a short walk. The office, the wharf, the shack,
the alley, the sluice, and the landing can be revisited. Facts change what
they say. The lane with the book shut is the only way into the last choice,
and it is three screens: alley, sluice, landing. Reading the hour on the
dock, under the lamp, or to the sluice spends it and ends the night.

Two captions that lead to the same page are one choice. Holding the ledger
replaces the circles; both sets are never on screen together. On the wharf
the open book offers the lane and the fog at once, so those two circles use
two anchors.

## The plot, as played

Edith is already seated. She asks for the hour, in this room, and for the
floor at berth four to stay shut. She leaves. The folder is Thomas Voss, tide
clerk, twenty-eight, berth four, the trawler Patience. Her pencil in the
margin: the hour, to her, in this room. Not to the water. Not in the rain.
Until that page has been read, the office door is not offered.

The window is optional and worth taking. A man in a wet wool coat stands under
the lamp, too still for the rain. The hand finds the latch. The pavement is
empty.

The door takes the hat from the hook. The rain goes down the hill to berth
four. The Patience knocks the pilings. The shack window is lit. The lane home
is a guess in the fog.

At the water, once, something pale turns between the pilings, the size of a
face, and the tide takes it. A second look finds nothing. The name is in the
book.

Marsh keeps his clear eye on you and the clouded one on the shack. The book is
on the table. Leave the floor. Read the hour to the woman up the hill. The
rain is not a room. He will not repeat the warning as news. If the ledger is
in hand, he puts a palm between the book and the deck and sends it up the hill.
The hour is not spent.

In the shack: a lamp, a table, the ledger blotched with tidewater, a rope in
the boards. Taking the book shows the dent of the name Thomas Voss, the dent
of an hour already passed, and her line under it. The trap, before or after
the take, opens on black water and the face from the folder.

On the wharf with the book in the coat, holding it open replaces the doors
with the mouths: the water, or the fog. The water keeps the face. The fog
takes the words, and a man in a wet coat keeps a few planks ahead. Pass him
and the climb reaches Edith, but she does not open the locket: the hour was
spoken in the rain, and he is still on the stair. Go to him and the face from
the folder looks back. He does not speak, and the hill does not take you.
Either way the book is still in the coat, and trying again returns to the
wharf where it was opened.

The lane with the book shut is three screens. The alley holds Abel Penry in
a lit doorway, the tide board, the bakery window, and the bridge. The board
is a wash. Penry, once the board has been read, says he washed the name off:
chalk washes off, and a dry room does not. A woman draws the bakery blind.
The sluice is a footbridge. Looking down finds nothing. A peg in the grating
is cut VOSS. Holding the book and reading to that water spends the hour.
The landing is the streetlamp and the office door. If the office window was
taken, he is under the lamp until he turns and goes down into the fog.
Reading the hour there spends it. The hall has green light under the office
door. Then the dry room, which is the visit plate: Edith is back in the chair.

The dry room has the folder on the blotter. The window is rain. Holding the
ledger and choosing the folder reads the hour there. If the lamp, the face,
and Marsh were all met, Edith closes the locket, the streetlamp stays empty,
and the floor does not answer. If any of those were skipped, she still closes
the locket, the rain is only rain, and the floor at berth four stays shut.
Both are wins. Choosing the glass instead reads the hour to the pane. The man
under the lamp steps in close, and the latch rises under a hand that is not
yours. The door, with the book shut, goes back to the wharf.

## What the four clues are

A reader who has these can solve the night. Each one is in the prose.

1. Edith, in the chair: read the hour to her in this room, and leave the floor shut.
2. The folder margin: to her, in this room. Not to the water. Not in the rain.
3. Marsh: the book is on the table, the floor answers, the rain is not a room, carry it up the hill.
4. The ledger: his name, an hour already passed, and her line. Say it where the floor is dry.

The hint, if someone is stuck on the dock with the book: her pencil said where.
The lane with the book shut is the hill. Holding the book open spends the hour
on whatever is touched. On the way home Penry says the same thing in other
words. Chalk washes off. A dry room does not. The board, that sentence, and
the peg change the road's lines. They are not required for either win.

room, and read it onto the folder.

## Opening

Edith is already in the chair. The strange event is the rule itself, and the
man under the lamp if the window is taken. The first decision is the empty
office: folder, window, and, after the folder, the door.

## Contrast

Warm: Edith, the green lamp, the locket closing. Beautiful: rain on the glass,
the lit shack, the black water. Cold: the man who is too still, the face, the
latch. The one dry joke is Marsh's palm.

The hidden sight is the face between the pilings. The shortcut is the quiet
win: folder, ledger, lane, folder, without the window, the water, or Marsh.
The kinder night is the best ending, which remembers that the streetlamp was
occupied and is now empty.

## Film grammar

The plate is what the detective sees. A clip is that same view in motion.
Other people are in the shot. He stays the camera.

Clips live in `clips/`. Each is a few seconds, 3:2, at least five seconds in
the file the test measures, and 720×480 when played. The camera JPEG is the
poster. Headless play stays on the poster.

- `visit` — Edith looks into the lens and gives the rule.
- `window` — the man under the lamp, then empty cobbles. The second look is a still.
- `marsh` — the mate turns his eye, the first time only. His answers stay on the still.
- `water` — the face between the pilings. The second look is a still. The harbor ending plays it again.
- `below` — the face rising under the floor.
- `street` — the lane, and a step that matches his, when the hour is read to the fog.
- `bakery` — she draws the blind, and stays behind the glass.
- `lamp` — Thomas, under the landing lamp, turns and walks down into the fog.
- `penry`, `penry-voss`, `penry-wash`, `penry-rail` — Abel Penry speaks to the lens from his doorway.
- `marsh-voss`, `marsh-floor`, `marsh-boat`, `marsh-dry` — Marsh speaks to the lens from the deck. The approach clip `marsh` stays the first meeting.

Rain on an empty place is a still. The landing itself stays a still; the
clip plays only when he leaves.
These also stay on the still: the office door, the telephone, the green lamp,
Marsh's refusal, the shut-book lane, both wins, the glass, the dull walk
home, the bend, the turned face, Penry's answers, the board, a look down at
the sluice, the peg, the hall, and the locket. `frames/` holds reference

A new clip is first person, shows a person or a place already in the stills,

## Plates and prose

`rooms/grayhaven.blks` is a projection rig. Each location camera sits at eye
height and looks north. Anchors lie on a plane four metres in front of it.
The JPEGs in `rooms/` are the pictures and stay as they are. Missing art must
not display another room.

The street camera shares the wharf's position, so the street plate cannot take
a new decision. The wharf's anchors would land on it. A decision needs a
camera whose anchors match its own picture.

Reading regions, authored on the camera:

- Office, wharf, and shack decisions use the top band `0.05 0.03 0.56 0.22`.
- Alley, sluice, and landing decisions use `0.05 0.03 0.56 0.28`.
- Visit is the opening, the dry room, both wins, and the partial. Its band is
  `0.05 0.03 0.56 0.30`, above Edith.
- Marsh's answers use the fog at the right, `0.48 0.04 0.47 0.40`, so his
  face, the boat, and the deck stay clear of the prose.
- The folder, the window, the water, the ledger, the trap, the lane, and the
  coat use the lower band `0.06 0.68 0.52 0.22`. Board, bakery, hall, and the
  close-ups (`office-phone`, `office-lamp`, `locket`, `shack-coat`, `penry`,
  `sluice-grate`, `landing-window`, `lamp`) use a taller lower band, because
  those plates have no circles.

Type stays at `textScale` 1. If a beat needs more words, give it another page
or a second region. Do not shrink the type. Choice labels stay short noun
phrases, under about thirty characters: "The folder", "The lane", "The
trapdoor". Spending the hour uses the same anchor with a "To the…" label:
"To the folder", "To the glass", "To the water", "To the fog".

Circles need a clear radius of space between their edges, and they stay out of
the prose. The carried ledger is a round icon at the lower left. Keep that
corner free on decision cameras.

## Continuing the story

This night is complete. Continue it by a later page that still obeys the rule,
or by another street that earns a new plate. Do not rewrite the hour into a
different puzzle.

Keep:

- The hour is a summons. The dry room, onto her folder, is the fair mouth.
  The glass, the rain, the harbor, the lamp, the sluice, and the floor are
  mouths that answer. The board, Penry, and the peg do not join the best ending.
- After the best or the quiet win, the streetlamp is empty and the floor stays
  shut. Thomas does not climb back through that trap on the same night.
- The partial night is the one where something matches his step. A continuation
  from that ending starts in the fog, still carrying the book, and says so.
- A failure continuation starts from the retry the ending already names, with
  the book where it was.
- A new screen is a still with at least two circles, three if it is a hub.
  Nothing is written over that still. A shot returns by itself: the clip ends,
  or the picture is tapped. Continue is only for a document, the way a file is
  put down. Do not put the walk home back into a chain of clips.
- Edith does not go to the dock. Marsh does not come up to the office. Thomas
  does not explain the tide.
- One ledger. Facts only turn on. New facts are few, named, and written into
  `work/STORY_STATE.md` before they appear in `title.xml`.
- Second person, the voice above, and the four clues left intact. A new clue
  can sharpen them. It must not contradict them.
- Crowe stays the camera, in stills and in clips.
- Existing JPEGs stay. A new room gets its own camera, its own still, and its
  own anchors. A document camera keeps a `textRect` for the page being read.
  A hub does not.

A new page, in order:

1. Write the beat in `work/MAP.md`. If it changes the hour or adds a mouth,
   update `work/PUZZLES.md` and this file's ending table.
2. Make the picture before the page, if the beat needs one. Match the
   existing plates: eye height, rain, the green lamp indoors, lanterns on the
   dock, 3:2.
3. Add the page to its location in `locations/` (a new location also goes
   in the node in `title.xml`), in the forms already used there and described
   in [authoring/XML.md](../../authoring/XML.md). Facts are the lowercase
   boolean variables declared in `title.xml` (`SEEN` in these notes is
   `seen`). A clip is a bare file name under `books/salt/clips/`. Action lists
   (the old checks) are invisible and follow on their own.
4. Add the route to [WALKTHROUGH.md](WALKTHROUGH.md) and `tests/test_salt.py`.
   Tests tap Continue only on a document. A shot is finished with `:continue`,
   which is the clip ending. Tests tap retry and hold the same way the player does.
5. Run `build/cli/book check salt` and `build/cli/book graph salt`, then
   `make check`. If a document overflows its reading region, shorten it.

Choice labels for a new decision stay in the same dialect. Set the option up
in the sentence above it. A decision has at least two circles. A hub has at
least three, and no screen shows more than five. The office has five after
the folder. The wharf has four. Holding the ledger replaces the circles with
the readings that screen allows. If it allows none, the circles return when
the book is shut. A screen that can spend the hour keeps a way off it while
the book is open.

## Exclusions

The engine has no parser, no dice, no timer, no score, and no sound. This book
also has no inventory list, no second carried tool, no combat, and no typed
command. "Try again" is the retry on a failure or a partial ending. "Start
again" is the success button. Circles are the choices, except Penry's talk,
which uses lines.
