# The Salt Ledger

Grayhaven, 1924. A harbor detective story told as a picture book with film clips.

## Dungeon report

- Full case, built from the ZILScript story steps: premise, world inventory, puzzle with wrong answers, state ledger, then the page table.
- Stills stay the first-person plates already in `rooms/`. Clips stay first person too. The detective is the camera, not a figure in the shot.
- Win is concrete: carry the ledger to the office and read Thomas's hour onto Edith's folder. The floor, the rain, and the harbor are the wrong mouths for that hour.

## Premise

Thomas Voss, a tide clerk, went down to berth four and did not come back. His sister Edith hires the detective to find the hour written beside Thomas's name and to read it aloud in her dry room. The harbor ledger is in Caleb Marsh's shack. Under the shack floor is the water, and Thomas answers if his hour is spoken where the water can hear it.

What is wrong here:

- Thomas is not lost at sea. He stands under the streetlamp, turns under the pilings, and waits beneath the floor.
- The hour in the ledger is a summons. Spoken in the rain, on the dock, or under the floor, it calls him. Spoken in the office, to Edith, it lets the tide go out unanswered.
- Marsh will say this. He will not touch the book, and he will not go down the trap.

## Core fantasy

Look, listen, and decide where a dead man's name is allowed to be spoken.

## Audience and tone

One reader, about half an hour, replayed for the ending they missed. Mystery first, with a cold supernatural turn. Prose is short and concrete. Danger is real and not gory. Marsh is dry. Edith is warm. The harbor at night is the beauty.

## Film grammar

The plate on a page is what the detective sees: first person. A clip is that same view in motion. Other people are in the shot. He is not. The door, the reading in the dry room, and the reading at the glass stay on the still.

## How it ends

- **Best:** read the hour onto the folder after seeing the lamp, the face in the water, and hearing Marsh. The streetlamp stays empty.
- **Quiet:** read it onto the folder with only the note and the ledger. The floor stays shut. The rain is only rain.
- **Partial:** read the hour to the lane. Something keeps your pace in the fog.
- **Failures:** leave with empty hands; open the trap; read the hour to the harbor; read it to the wet glass. Each one says what you did.

## Opening

Edith is already in the chair. She states the rule. The folder repeats it. The door down to the harbor stays shut until the folder has been read. The first reward is the berth, the boat, and her pencil in the margin.

## Plates

`rooms/grayhaven.blks` is a projection rig. Each location camera sits at eye height and looks north. Anchors lie on a plane four metres in front of it. The JPEGs are the pictures and are unchanged.

Clips live in `clips/`. Each is a few seconds, 3:2, and first person:

- `visit` — Edith gives him the rule.
- `window` — the man under the lamp, then empty cobbles.
- `marsh` — the mate on the deck.
- `water` — the face between the pilings.
- `below` — the face rising under the floor.
- `street` — the lane, and a step that matches his.
