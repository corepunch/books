# Puzzles

> Written for the screens before the X-Files structure. [GDD.md](../GDD.md) is the
> game now; this file is kept for its reasoning.

The case is one puzzle. The hour is a summons. The only fair place to speak
it is the dry room, onto Edith's folder. Everything else is a mouth he can
answer.

## Speak the hour in the dry room

- Goal: ending-best or ending-quiet.
- Gate: READ (her pencil) and the ledger carried. The best ending also wants
  SEEN, GLIMPSED, and HEARD. Those three are not a lock. Skipping them still
  wins. It wins more quietly. BOARD, PENRY, and PEG are not a lock either.
- Clues, in the order a reader meets them:
  1. Edith, in the chair: read the hour to her in this room, and leave the floor shut.
  2. The folder margin: to her, in this room. Not to the water. Not in the rain.
  3. Marsh, asked about the floor: the book is on the table, the rain is not a room.
  4. The ledger: his name, an hour already passed, and her line. Say it where the floor is dry.
- Solution: take the ledger, keep it shut through the alley, the sluice, and
  the landing, and in the dry room hold it and choose the folder.
- Softlock: none. The dry room's door returns to the wharf. The folder, if
  the book is shut, says to open it. The alley and the sluice walk both ways.
  Retry on a failure restores the decision, the facts, and the book.

## Wrong attempts

Each is a choice the picture and the text already named.

| Attempt | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Open the trap before taking the book | Shack | The face from the folder rises. He did not need the hour. |
| Open the trap after taking it | Shack | The face rises because the name was brought down to the water. |
| Read the hour to the harbor | Wharf, ledger held, the water | The face turns up and stays. |
| Read the hour to the lane, then pass him | Wharf, ledger held, the fog, then the hill | Edith does not open the locket. He waits on the stair. Partial. |
| Read the hour to the lane, then go to him | The same bend, the coat | The face looks back and does not speak. The hill does not take you. |
| Read the hour to the glass | Dry room, ledger held, the window | The latch rises from outside. |
| Read the hour to the sluice | Footbridge, ledger held, the water | A pale face turns up. Retry returns to that bridge. |
| Read the hour under the lamp | Landing, ledger held, the lamp | He is beside you before the line is done. |
| Look at the sluice without reading | Footbridge, the water | No face. The stair is still there. |
| Leave the dock with empty hands | Wharf, the lane, before the book | A dull morning. The folder is still on the desk. |
| Read it on Marsh's deck | Wharf or his deck, book open | He puts a palm between the book and the boat. The hour is not spent. |
| Look at the water a second time | Wharf | The harbor keeps what it turned. The name is in the book. |
| Choose the folder with the book shut | Dry room | The page tells you to open the ledger. The case does not end. |

Hint if someone is stuck on the dock with the book: her pencil said where.
The lane with the book shut is the hill. Holding it open spends the hour on
whatever is touched. Penry says the same thing once the board has been read.
Chalk washes off. A dry room does not.

There is no second key and no timed clock. The tide has already passed. The
hour does not expire while the reader thinks.
